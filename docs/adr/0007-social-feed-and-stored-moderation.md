# Swiftter becomes a social feed; moderation decisions are stored, and publishing fails closed with a pending state

Swiftter only had Posts. It now has threaded replies, reshares and a paginated feed, and every moderation decision is kept. The owner chose (2026-09-30) to store refused notes for their author instead of discarding them (superseding ADR-0006's "nothing is stored"). The design went through three adversarial reviews (schema, failure mode, auth); their outcomes are in [the session log](../sessions/2026-09-30/SESSION-LOG.md).

- **Replies live in `posts`** (`parent_id`, `root_id`): a reply is a rich-text message like a Post, with the same sanitising, moderation, tear-up and limits. A stored generated `thread_id` and a composite foreign key make the database itself refuse a reply outside its parent's thread.
- **Reshares get their own table**: they carry no text, so they are neither moderated nor tombstoned. Unique per (Member, Post); undoing soft-deletes and resharing again restores the same row, so undo/redo neither bumps the feed nor frees a place in the limit. Self-reshares and reshares of anything but a public Post are refused by the insert itself.
- **Publishing fails closed in the schema**: `posts.status` defaults to `pending` and only `published_at` makes a note public; the feed sorts by it. A note is inserted pending first, then judged, so a Gateway failure or a crash never loses it.
- **Moderation failure mode: fail-closed with a stored pending state** (debated against fail-open, which lost: it cannot satisfy "moderated before public", and anyone who can make the model return no verdict would get exactly the worst content published). A note with no verdict is kept, shown to its author ("waiting for a check") and re-checked by "check again" (offered for its first 4 attempts) and by an hourly Vercel Cron job: at every run for its first day, then daily, then a last time once it is a week old (`MODERATION_RETRY`, computed from its `moderation_decisions` rows), so an outage of a few hours strands nothing. Only then is it given up on: its author is told plainly, and the run that gave up logs one alert. (Amended 2026-09-30, #113; it was every 15 minutes, 4 attempts in all, which an hour-long outage used up.) Refused notes are kept blocked, visible to their author only, with the reason.
- **Retention** (added 2026-09-30, #115): a torn-up note shows no author at all (no name, id or avatar, in the page, its JSON-LD or the thread's data). Once a day the cron purges tombstones nothing refers to, refused notes and notes given up on after 30 days, and the model's reason on approved decisions after 30 days.
- **Reports, appeals and owner alerts** (added 2026-10-01, #116, #117): Members can "Report" someone else's public note and "Ask a human to look again" at their own refused one; both are rows of `note_reports` (one per Member, note and kind, with an optional plain-text reason, rate-limited like other writes for reports), and go with their note. The hourly cron sums up the unsent ones in one owner alert per run (not one per click) and marks them sent only once it went out. Owner alerts (this summary and the notes given up on) open, or comment on, a GitHub issue through the REST API when `OWNER_ALERTS_GITHUB_TOKEN` is set, and are logged otherwise. The repository is public, so an alert carries note ids and links to public threads only: who reported, why, and what a held note says stay in the database. Tearing a note up settles its reports, deleting an account removes the Member's own; resolved reports are purged after 30 days.
- **Moderators** (added 2026-10-01, #166): reports and appeals are handled in the app, on `/guestbook/moderation`, by moderators: Members with a row in `moderators` (a table rather than a column, so each grant says when and by whom; no list of ids in an env var, so adding one needs no redeploy), granted with `npm run moderator` or, on production, the Grant moderator workflow. A moderator tears a note up (the app's own tear-up), keeps it, or publishes a refused one after all; each decision is appended to `moderation_decisions` with `human:<member id>` as its model and the moderator's optional note as its reason (which the 30-day purge of the model's reasons leaves alone: it is the audit), settles the note's open reports and appeals, and refreshes the feed. The role is checked by the page (404 for anyone else), by every route (403) and again in the service, inside the decision's transaction, which also holds the note's row, so two moderators deciding at once take turns and the second is told it was already handled. A moderator's note never reaches the author (their held note keeps showing the model's reason), and their export says a human decided, not which one.
- **Account deletion** (added 2026-09-30, #112): a Member deletes their account from their guestbook page (`/guestbook`), which also downloads their data. Their notes are torn up (threads stay readable), their reshares deleted, their Member row emptied, and their Neon Auth account deleted straight from `neon_auth."user"` in the same transaction: Neon Auth's hosted `delete-user` endpoint is disabled (404), so this is a second deliberate exception to ADR-0004, as narrow as the first (one row, by the session's own user id). (Amended 2026-10-01, #83: the app's database role can no longer reach `neon_auth`; it deletes that row through `public.delete_auth_user`, [ADR-0008](0008-least-privilege-database-role.md).)
- **Every attempt is appended to `moderation_decisions`** (outcome, category, reason, model, duration): auditable, and reversible by hand (set the note approved and published). Tearing a note up erases its text and its reasons.
- **Deleting always tombstones**; a best-effort purge removes tombstones nothing refers to once they no longer count towards a limit. Threads and reshares show a torn-up note as such.
- **Counts are computed when read**, never stored, so concurrent replies and reshares cannot drift them; the feed is one keyset-paginated SQL query per page (no N+1, tested).
- **Every write goes through one wrapper** (`lib/member-write.ts`): same-origin only, JSON only, session (503 when Neon Auth fails, not a false 401), BotID; then ownership and visibility in SQL (404). Limits per kind: Posts 5, replies 10, reshares 10 per 10 minutes.
- **Development fixtures are marked `is_seed`** (separate from `is_demo`, the demo content the owner shows on the live site) and live on the `dev` Neon branch only; `scripts/seed.ts` and `scripts/unseed.ts` refuse production. Unseed deletes the seed's own Neon Auth accounts directly in the branch's `neon_auth` schema, by their exact addresses: a deliberate, guarded exception to ADR-0004.
- **Rollback**: the migration is additive; `drizzle/down/0002_swiftter_social.down.sql` reverts it and must run before production is rolled back to code older than it (such code would show replies and held notes as ordinary Posts).
